Episode 35. Economic Technocrats.
Today: who economic technocrats are, why politicians make bad economic policy, and why technocrats might do better.

First, who they are: central bankers, finance officials and leaders of institutions like the IMF and World Bank, people like Jerome Powell, Christine Lagarde and Kristalina Georgieva. They typically hold economics PhDs, often come from finance, and trust data-driven policy.

Second, why politicians have bad incentives. Elections every few years push them toward short-term fixes, like Malaysia's capital controls in 1997, which hurt long-term investment. They will not cross powerful constituencies, which is why pension reform stalls against older voters and India's parties avoid farm reform. Populist pressure drives spending on popular but inefficient programs. They refuse to admit failure, propping up zombie banks as Japan did in its lost decade. They are captured by lobbyists and donors. Voters are often misinformed and biased, and good economics rarely makes a good soundbite. Fragile coalitions require pork-barrel spending. Politicians fear looking inconsistent or breaking party doctrine. Democracies are often unrepresentative, through low turnout, gerrymandering and lobbying. And leaders can be arrogant and economically incompetent, firing advisors who disagree, like Erdogan's disastrous management of Turkey's economy.

Third, why technocrats have better incentives. They care about long-term legacy and face no election. Good management protects their future careers in the private sector. They come from a culture of research, debate and self-criticism, and they have real experience rather than being chosen for charisma or campaign money.

Finally, other benefits. Technocrats act faster in crises, since economists' disagreements are narrower than partisan or ethnic ones. Giving them fiscal as well as monetary power would align both. Policy becomes more stable across changes of government. And foreign investors trust skilled economists more than unpredictable politicians, which brings in investment.
