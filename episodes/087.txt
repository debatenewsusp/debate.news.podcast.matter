Episode 87. Securities Firms.
Today: a quick map of the financial world: investment banks, private equity, venture capital, hedge funds and asset managers.

First, investment banks serve big companies, wealthy people and other banks. They underwrite bonds, assessing creditworthiness and helping firms issue debt; run IPOs when companies go public; advise on mergers and acquisitions; and manage wealth for rich clients. The biggest, like Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and Bank of America, are called bulge bracket banks; smaller specialists are boutiques.

Second, private equity. Stocks are public equity; shares in companies not listed on exchanges are private equity. PE firms raise money from accredited investors and buy stakes in private companies, often through leveraged buyouts, borrowing to buy control. They work with management to raise the company's value and sell after seven to ten years. Big names include Blackstone, Apollo and Carlyle.

Third, venture capital works the same way but bets on risky start-ups. Most investments fail, but one unicorn can pay for all the losses, so VCs take large ownership stakes and help guide growth.

Fourth, hedge funds pool money from wealthy investors and institutions to try to beat the market, using complex instruments and heavy borrowing. They face lighter regulation and disclosure rules, and whether they actually earn better returns is hotly debated.

Fifth, asset managers, like mutual funds and pension funds, invest on behalf of ordinary people who lack time or expertise. They use bonds, which pay a fixed return; stocks, which are more volatile but rewarding when diversified; and derivatives, which are bets on underlying assets, like Treasury futures. Strategies differ by risk appetite, from families wanting stability to millionaires speculating, and by liquidity, since long-term savers can hold assets that are hard to sell quickly.
