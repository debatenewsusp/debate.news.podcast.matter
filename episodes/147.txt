Episode 147. Australia.
Today: Australia's politics, its security alliances, Indigenous history and its economy.

First, politics. Australia is a federation of six states plus territories, governed from Canberra. In 2022, Anthony Albanese's center-left Labor Party ended nearly a decade of the center-right Liberal-National Coalition, which was hurt by scandals, pork-barreling, the unpopularity of Scott Morrison and Robodebt, an automated welfare debt system that wrongly pursued thousands of people. The Greens and independents, known as the crossbench, also matter in parliament.

Second, security. Australia belongs to AUKUS, with the U.S. and Britain, under which it will acquire nuclear-powered submarines, and the Quad, with the U.S., India and Japan. It is building security ties in the Pacific, including with Papua New Guinea, to counter China's influence, after China signed a security pact with the Solomon Islands.

Third, Indigenous Australians. Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, about three percent of the population, have lived there for over sixty thousand years. Colonization brought frontier massacres, deadly diseases and land dispossession, and up to one in three Aboriginal children were forcibly removed from their families, the Stolen Generations. Australia formally apologized in 2008. Large gaps remain: Aboriginal people live about ten years less on average, are around fifteen times more likely to be imprisoned, and face higher unemployment, homelessness and violence.

Fourth, the economy. Australia is a rich, well-regulated economy with strong banks and vast resources, such as iron ore, coal, gas, bauxite and uranium. Services employ about three-quarters of workers, and China is its largest trading partner.

Since the file was written: in October 2023, Australians rejected a referendum to create an Indigenous Voice to Parliament, and Albanese won a landslide re-election in 2025.
