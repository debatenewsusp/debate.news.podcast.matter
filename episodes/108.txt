Episode 108. China's Military Modernization.
Today: why China is building up its military, how it is modernizing, and the structure of the People's Liberation Army.

First, why. With the PLA's centenary in 2027, Xi Jinping ties military power directly to the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation; as a 2015 white paper put it in effect, no country can be strong without a strong military. The reasons: the Century of Humiliation taught the party the price of weakness. A rising power needs force to control its borders, from Xinjiang to Hong Kong, press territorial claims in the South China Sea, and protect Chinese citizens and businesses abroad. It prepares for possible conflict with the U.S. or an invasion of Taiwan. Military strength brings prestige and patriotism, from aircraft carriers to national day parades. Expansion keeps the army loyal and alert, paired with harsh anti-corruption purges. And it supports economic strategies like Made in China 2025. Spending is roughly two hundred and fifty billion dollars a year, and a Pentagon official warned China is acquiring weapons five times faster than the U.S.

Second, how. Xi, as head of the Central Military Commission, has three aims: mechanize and informationize the army with modern communications; intelligentize it by bringing AI into command and weapons; and restructure the PLA so it can fight and win wars. China has replaced Cold War equipment with stealth J-20 fighters, three aircraft carriers, the Liaoning, Shandong and Fujian, upgraded bombers carrying cruise missiles, homegrown air defense missiles, new tanks and electronic warfare aircraft.

Third, structure. The army has close to a million active soldiers. The navy has around three hundred and forty ships and submarines, the world's largest by number. The air force has about two thousand eight hundred aircraft. The Rocket Force controls intercontinental ballistic missiles, and a separate force handles cyber and space operations, alongside a logistics force. China is estimated to have several hundred nuclear warheads, and the number is growing.

That was China's Military Modernization. Next episode: PLA Invasion of Taiwan.
