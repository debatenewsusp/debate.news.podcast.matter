Episode 130. DRC.
Today: decades of war in the Democratic Republic of Congo and the M23 rebellion.

First, history. After independence in 1960, Congo fell into civil war: a mutiny against white officers, a power struggle ending in the assassination of Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba, and secession attempts in Katanga and South Kasai. Mobutu Sese Seko took power in 1965 and ruled as a corrupt autocrat.

Second, the Congo wars. After the 1994 Rwandan genocide, about two million mostly Hutu Rwandans fled into Congo, then called Zaire, including the extremists responsible. Mobutu let them attack Rwanda and persecuted Congolese Tutsis. In 1996, Paul Kagame's Rwanda invaded with Uganda, toppling Mobutu and installing Laurent-Désiré Kabila. When Kabila turned on his backers, Rwanda invaded again in 1998, and Angola, Namibia and Zimbabwe joined on Kabila's side, a conflict called Africa's World War. It ended in 2003 under his son Joseph Kabila, but violence continued in the eastern Kivu and Ituri provinces.

Third, M23. In 2009 the Tutsi-led CNDP rebels made peace, joining the army with their own command and mining access. The deal collapsed, and in 2012 former CNDP soldiers mutinied as the March 23 Movement, M23, briefly capturing Goma before UN and Congolese forces pushed them back. M23 relaunched an offensive in 2022 under President Félix Tshisekedi.

Fourth, the international dimension. Rwanda is credibly accused of arming and funding M23. Uganda and Rwanda profit from Congolese minerals, Chinese state firms own most cobalt and copper mines, and Russia supplies weapons to the army. The army also works with the FDLR, a Hutu militia hostile to Rwanda, and an Islamic State affiliate, the ADF, adds to the violence. Over seven million people are displaced. As the UN wound down its peacekeepers, East African and southern African forces were deployed.

Since the file was written: M23 captured Goma and Bukavu in early 2025. The U.S. brokered a peace agreement between Congo and Rwanda in mid-2025, linked to mineral deals, and Qatar mediated talks with M23, but fighting has continued.
