Episode 67. Jerome Powell.
Today: Powell's background, his path to the Fed, his record, and his 2023 Jackson Hole message.

First, background. Powell studied politics at Princeton and law at Georgetown, then worked in investment banking. He served in the first Bush administration's Treasury, overseeing financial institutions and debt markets, then became a partner at the Carlyle Group and ran investment funds, before working on the 2011 debt-ceiling crisis at the Bipartisan Policy Center. Notably, he is a lawyer and financier, not an economics PhD.

Second, his rise. Obama, a Democrat, appointed Powell, a lifelong Republican, to the Fed's board in 2012. Trump nominated him as chair in 2017, and the Senate confirmed him with a large bipartisan majority.

Third, his record. He kept raising rates and shrinking the Fed's balance sheet in 2018, to Trump's fury, then reversed course in 2019. When COVID hit, he launched an unprecedented emergency response. When inflation surged from 2021, he raised rates eleven times to between five and a quarter and five and a half percent.

Fourth, the 2023 Jackson Hole speech. Pandemic demand collided with constrained supply, so the Fed tightened. Inflation fell sharply from its 2022 peak, but core inflation was still above four percent, and two good months were not enough. Goods inflation eased as car supply recovered and auto loan rates doubled. Housing inflation was cooling slowly because leases reset gradually. Services inflation, in labor-intensive sectors like healthcare and restaurants, was barely moving because the job market stayed tight. Tightening was working: real rates rose, lending standards tightened, and industrial production and housing investment slowed. But spending and hiring remained strong. His bottom line: two percent is the target. Doing too little lets inflation become entrenched and forces more painful tightening later; doing too much causes needless economic damage.
