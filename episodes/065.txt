Episode 65. China's Economic Slowdown.
Today: the symptoms of China's slowdown as of 2023, its causes, and reasons not to panic.

First, the symptoms. Youth unemployment passed twenty percent. Prices fell into deflation, bank lending slumped, imports and exports dropped double digits, and foreign investment hit a twenty-five-year low. Smaller cities hold millions of unsold apartments and prices are collapsing, while developers like Evergrande and Country Garden teeter. The post-COVID rebound lasted barely a quarter, and Beijing began restricting economic data.

Second, seven causes. One, zero-COVID lockdowns crushed production and demand and pushed wasteful infrastructure spending; growth in 2022 was under three percent. Two, the global economy: high energy prices and rate hikes in the West cut demand. Three, geopolitics: decoupling, chip export controls and tariff wars with the U.S. and EU. Four, Xi Jinping's crackdown on the private sector, even though private firms produce most of China's output, innovation and jobs. Jack Ma vanished from view after criticizing regulators, Ant Group's IPO was blocked, Alibaba was fined billions, tutoring companies were gutted, and Didi was pulled from app stores. Five, the growth model ran out: like Japan and Singapore before it, China grew through very high savings funneled into investment, while consumption is unusually low at under forty percent of GDP. Eventually investment outpaced productive uses, causing bubbles, rising debt and loans to inefficient state firms, made worse by state banks funding white elephants. Six, demographics: after the one-child policy, the population is shrinking and was overtaken by India, the birth rate is far below replacement, and an aging society strains pensions, with workers retiring around sixty. Seven, real estate makes up roughly a quarter of the economy and is drowning in debt, with ghost cities as symbols of overbuilding.

Third, reasons for optimism. Before real crises, bank shares usually collapse, as in the U.S. in 2008, but Chinese bank stocks stayed stable. Chinese stock markets recovered from late 2022, global commodity prices did not signal a Chinese collapse, the currency recovered against regional peers, tourism to Macau returned to pre-pandemic levels, car sales stayed strong, and Alibaba posted record results. So fears of imminent collapse may be overblown.
