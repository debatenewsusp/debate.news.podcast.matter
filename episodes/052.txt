Episode 52. Life of Jesus.
Today: the life of Jesus as told in the Gospels, from birth to resurrection.

First, birth and early ministry. After the angel Gabriel's announcement to Mary, Jesus was born in Bethlehem, hailed as the Messiah and Son of God. The Magi brought gold, frankincense and myrrh, and King Herod, fearing a rival king, ordered the Massacre of the Innocents. Jesus was baptized by John the Baptist, and the Holy Spirit descended as a voice declared Him God's beloved Son. He then fasted forty days in the desert and resisted the Devil's temptations.

Second, the ministry. Preaching in Galilee, He chose twelve apostles of humble origins, including Peter, Andrew, James, John, Matthew, Thomas and Judas Iscariot, and delivered the Sermon on the Mount. When Peter declared Him the Messiah, Jesus called Peter the rock on which He would build His church and gave him the keys of the Kingdom, a passage many see as the basis of the papacy. The Gospels describe miracles: turning water into wine, healing the sick and blind, exorcisms, raising Lazarus, feeding multitudes and walking on water. He taught through parables: the sower, about faith rooted in understanding; the mustard seed, about God's Kingdom growing; the prodigal son, about mercy; the rich fool, about greed; and the Good Samaritan, about showing mercy to everyone. At the Transfiguration He shone with light as Moses and Elijah appeared, marking Him as God's messenger.

Third, the Passion. He entered Jerusalem on a donkey to crowds waving palms, then drove the money-changers from the Temple, calling it a den of thieves. At the Last Supper He predicted Judas's betrayal and Peter's three denials, shared bread and wine as His body and blood, the origin of the Eucharist, and commanded His disciples to love one another. He prayed in agony at Gethsemane, then Judas identified Him to soldiers with a kiss. When a disciple drew a sword, Jesus warned that those who live by the sword die by it.

Fourth, trial and crucifixion. Tried before the Jewish council and then the Roman governor Pontius Pilate, He was accused of blasphemy and of claiming to be King of the Jews. Meanwhile Peter denied knowing Him three times before the rooster crowed. Pilate hesitated, but the crowd demanded the release of Barabbas instead. Jesus was mocked and flogged, carried His cross along the Via Dolorosa to Calvary, and was crucified between two criminals, asking God to forgive His killers and finally commending His spirit to the Father.

Finally, the resurrection. Three days later, Mary Magdalene found the tomb empty. Jesus appeared to His disciples and gave the Great Commission, to make disciples of all nations, before ascending to heaven.
