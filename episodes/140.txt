Episode 140. East Timor.
Today: Asia's youngest democracy and its oil dilemma.

First, history. East Timor, also called Timor-Leste, was a Portuguese colony until 1975. Days after declaring independence, it was invaded and annexed by Indonesia, and the occupation that followed was brutal. In 1999, a UN-sponsored referendum showed overwhelming support for independence, the UN administered the territory, and it became independent in 2002. Relations with Indonesia have since normalized.

Second, the economy. East Timor is one of the least developed countries, uses the U.S. dollar, and depends on oil and gas. Its main producing field, Bayu-Undan, is nearly exhausted. Its Petroleum Fund, worth about eighteen billion dollars, pays for over three-quarters of government spending, so the fund is being drawn down. Hopes rest on developing the Greater Sunrise gas field in the Timor Sea with Australia's Woodside, and on diversifying, helped by a new undersea fiber-optic cable to Australia.

Third, politics. Politics is largely stable. In 2023, the party of independence hero Xanana Gusmão won elections, making him prime minister, alongside President José Ramos-Horta, a Nobel Peace laureate.

Fourth, foreign policy. Australia is its key partner, though they have clashed over maritime borders and oil. East Timor has also used its voice on Myanmar, hosting the exiled opposition government.

Since the file was written: in October 2025, East Timor became the eleventh member of ASEAN.
