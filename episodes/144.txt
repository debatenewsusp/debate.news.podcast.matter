Episode 144. Malaysia.
Today: Malaysia's history, its turbulent coalition politics and its non-aligned foreign policy.

First, history. After Japan's wartime occupation, Britain organized the Federation of Malaya and fought a communist insurgency known as the Malayan Emergency, using harsh tactics including chemical defoliants. Malaya became independent in 1957, and in 1963 joined Singapore, Sabah and Sarawak to form Malaysia. Tensions between Malays and Singapore's Chinese majority, including race riots, led to Singapore's expulsion in 1965.

Second, institutions. Parliament has an upper house mostly appointed by the king, a rotating monarch, and a lower house elected by first past the post. There are three main blocs: Pakatan Harapan, the center-left coalition led by Anwar Ibrahim; Barisan Nasional, the right-wing coalition that ruled from independence until 2018; and Perikatan Nasional, a conservative Malay coalition led by Muhyiddin Yassin.

Third, the turmoil. Barisan Nasional held power for six decades through gerrymandering that favored rural voters, vote-buying, pressure through state-linked employers and corruption. In the 1MDB scandal, billions were looted from a state fund, with about seven hundred million dollars landing in Prime Minister Najib Razak's personal accounts. In 2018, voters ousted it, and ninety-two-year-old Mahathir Mohamad returned as prime minister, promising to hand power to Anwar. He never did. In 2020, defections known as the Sheraton Move collapsed the government, and Muhyiddin took over, then resigned in 2021 amid the COVID crisis, replaced by Ismail Sabri Yaakob. The 2022 election, the first with automatic registration and voting from age eighteen, adding about six million voters, produced a hung parliament. Anwar finally became prime minister at the head of a unity government, including his old rivals in Barisan Nasional.

Fourth, the economy. Malaysia exports oil, gas and electronics, is a global center of Islamic finance, aims to diversify and grow small firms through its industrial master plan, and is reducing its deficit.

Fifth, foreign policy. China is its biggest trading partner, while the U.S. underpins regional security, so Malaysia stays non-aligned: it joins China's Belt and Road and America's Indo-Pacific Economic Framework, criticizes AUKUS yet deepens security ties with Washington, and trains with China's military too.

Since the file was written: Najib Razak, jailed in 2022, was convicted again in the main 1MDB trial, and Malaysia chaired ASEAN in 2025, welcoming East Timor as a member.
