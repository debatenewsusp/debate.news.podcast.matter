Episode 112. Venezuela.
Today: Chávez's Bolivarian Revolution, why the economy collapsed, and Maduro's authoritarian turn.

First, Chávez, president from 1999 to 2013. His Bolivarian Revolution nationalized industries privatized in the nineties, imposed price controls and launched welfare programs called Bolivarian missions, cutting poverty sharply and reducing child mortality, with fiercely anti-U.S. rhetoric.

Second, why it went wrong. State firms were corrupt and mismanaged; the state food distributor was caught letting well over a hundred thousand tons of food rot. Everything depended on oil, since Venezuela has the world's largest reserves, which caused Dutch disease, a strong currency that killed other exports, and heavy borrowing on the assumption oil prices would stay high. Price controls made production unprofitable, causing shortages. And currency controls, combined with printing money, sent inflation soaring, created a black market for dollars, and starved businesses of the foreign currency needed to import inputs.

Third, Maduro. After Chávez died in 2013, Nicolás Maduro took over. In 2014 recession hit, and when oil prices crashed in 2015, the welfare model collapsed. The opposition won the National Assembly in 2015, so Maduro packed the Supreme Tribunal, which in 2017 tried to strip the Assembly of its powers. He then created a Constituent Assembly, boycotted by the opposition, which claimed legislative power. In 2018 he won a widely condemned election with opposition candidates banned. In January 2019 the National Assembly declared opposition leader Juan Guaidó interim president, recognized by around sixty countries, while Russia and China backed Maduro. A military uprising failed, sanctions deepened shortages, Maduro blocked humanitarian aid, and millions fled the country.

Since the file was written: opposition leader María Corina Machado won the 2023 primary but was barred from running. In the July 2024 election, the authorities declared Maduro the winner, while the opposition published tally sheets showing its candidate, Edmundo González, had won. Machado received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2025. And in January 2026, U.S. strikes on Venezuela ended with Maduro captured and taken to the United States to face charges.

That was Venezuela. Next episode: Iran.
