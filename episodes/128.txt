Episode 128. Ukraine.
Today: the roots of the conflict with Russia, up to Crimea.

First, divided loyalties. Ukraine was the second most populous Soviet republic and became independent in 1991. Its west is more Ukrainian-speaking and favors ties with the EU and NATO, while parts of the east are Russian-speaking and lean toward Moscow, so its foreign policy swung between the two.

Second, 2014. When pro-Russian President Viktor Yanukovych rejected an association agreement with the EU, mass protests erupted, and parliament removed him. Russia refused to recognize the new pro-Western government. It sent unmarked soldiers, the so-called little green men, to seize key sites in Crimea, held a rigged referendum and annexed the peninsula. In the eastern Donbas, Russian-backed separatists declared the Donetsk and Luhansk People's Republics, and heavy fighting followed.

Third, Putin's motives. Russia fears Western military expansion toward its border. Putin sees Ukraine and Crimea as historically Russian, calling Kyiv the mother of Russian cities, and wants to restore Soviet-era influence. He has demanded that NATO permanently close the door to Ukraine and pull forces out of Eastern Europe. Those demands set the stage for the full-scale invasion of February 2022.
