Episode 123. El Salvador and Bukele.
Today: Nayib Bukele's war on the gangs.

After gangs killed sixty-two people in a single day in March 2022, El Salvador declared a state of emergency and kept renewing it. It suspended freedom of assembly and the right to a lawyer, extended detention without charge, allowed surveillance of private communications, criminalized gang membership, lengthened sentences and lowered the age of criminal responsibility to twelve. Around seventy thousand people, nearly two percent of the population, were arrested, giving El Salvador one of the highest incarceration rates in the world, many held in a huge new mega-prison.

Murders fell dramatically, and Bukele's approval soared above eighty-five percent, even as human rights groups documented arbitrary arrests, abuse and deaths in custody. For debates, this is the classic case of security versus civil liberties, and of popular authoritarianism.

Since the file was written, Bukele won re-election in 2024 despite a constitutional ban on consecutive terms, and his model has inspired imitators across Latin America.
