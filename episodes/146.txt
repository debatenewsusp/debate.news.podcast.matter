Episode 146. Singapore.
Today: how a tiny port became one of the world's richest countries.

First, history. Britain colonized Singapore in the early nineteenth century. In 1963 it joined Malaysia, but race riots and anger at Malaysia's favoritism toward ethnic Malays, under the Malay nationalist UMNO party, led to its expulsion in 1965.

Second, the Lee Kuan Yew model. Under Lee Kuan Yew and his People's Action Party, Singapore grew explosively for decades. He was authoritarian but pragmatic: he built strong institutions, purged corruption, invested in infrastructure, public housing and education, and used Singapore's location as a trading hub. Openness to trade, foreign investment and immigration made it a manufacturing and financial center, state-backed financing supported shipbuilding, electronics and banking, and the compulsory Central Provident Fund gathered savings.

Third, today. Singapore is among the world's richest countries, with a diversified economy and a banking sector managing about two trillion dollars, though Hong Kong remains closer to Chinese finance. The PAP dominates, with strict limits on speech, but its base is aging, and it decriminalized sex between men in 2022 partly to connect with younger voters.

Fourth, foreign policy. With no natural resources, Singapore needs open trade and energy imports from its neighbors. It stays neutral between the great powers, relying on the U.S. for security and on China for economic ties.

Since the file was written: Lawrence Wong succeeded Lee Hsien Loong as prime minister in 2024, completing the transition to the fourth generation of leaders, and the PAP won the 2025 election comfortably.

That was Singapore. Next episode: Australia.
