Episode 101. Trump's Indictments.
Today: the four criminal cases Donald Trump faced in 2023, and how they ended.

First, New York: Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg charged Trump with thirty-four counts tied to Michael Cohen's hush-money payment to Stormy Daniels. Cohen, now a witness against him, strengthened the case, but critics called its legal theory novel.

Second, the classified documents case: special counsel Jack Smith charged Trump with keeping classified documents at Mar-a-Lago, misleading investigators and showing secret information to others. The evidence looked strong, but the judge, Aileen Cannon, was a Trump appointee who had ruled in his favor before.

Third, Georgia: an investigation into his pressure on the state's election officials in 2020, including asking Secretary of State Brad Raffensperger to find nearly twelve thousand votes.

Fourth, January 6: an investigation into his role in the Capitol attack, which led to a federal indictment.

Since then: in May 2024 a New York jury convicted Trump on all thirty-four counts, and he received an unconditional discharge in January 2025. Judge Cannon dismissed the documents case in 2024. After Trump won the November 2024 election, the federal cases were dropped, and the Georgia case collapsed after the prosecutor was disqualified.
