Episode 124. NATO.
Today: Ukraine's membership bid and Sweden's path into the alliance.

First, Ukraine. In July 2023, NATO leaders met in Vilnius, Lithuania, still split on Ukraine joining. The relationship goes back to the 1994 Budapest Memorandum, in which the U.S., Russia and Britain promised to respect Ukraine's sovereignty in exchange for giving up its Soviet-era nuclear weapons. Ukraine began the membership process in 2008, but progress has been slow. NATO's open door policy, based on Article 10 of its founding treaty, lets countries join if they have a market economy, a functioning democracy and civilian control of the military, and above all no ongoing territorial disputes. Members like the U.S., Germany and Turkey argue Ukraine is not ready, mainly because Article 5 would oblige every member to join the war against Russia if Ukraine entered before it ends. Still, more than twenty members back its bid.

Second, Sweden. Turkey long blocked Sweden's application, accusing Stockholm of lax counterterrorism and support for Kurdish groups that Ankara calls terrorists. Before Vilnius, Erdogan agreed to back Sweden in exchange for U.S. sales of F-16 jets, frozen since Turkey bought Russian S-400 missiles, and he also sought visa-free travel to Europe's Schengen area.

Since the file was written: Finland joined NATO in April 2023 and Sweden in March 2024, bringing the alliance to thirty-two members. Ukraine remains outside.
