Episode 53. Life of Prophet Muhammad.
Today: the life of the Prophet Muhammad as told in Islamic tradition.

First, early life. Muhammad was born around 570 in Mecca, into the Quraysh tribe. His father died before his birth and his mother when he was six, so he was raised by his uncle Abu Talib, head of the Hashim clan. At twenty-five he married Khadija, and their daughter Fatima, who married Ali, became a model for Muslim women.

Second, revelation. Muhammad often withdrew to pray in the mountains around Mecca, troubled by the moral ignorance of his society. In 610, on the Night of Destiny, the angel Gabriel appeared to him at Mount Hira and commanded him to recite, revealing the first verses of the Quran. Shaken, he was comforted by Khadija, who became the first believer. He preached privately at first, then from around 613 publicly: there is one God, idols are false, and judgment is coming.

Third, persecution. Mecca's polytheist elite mocked and then persecuted his followers, and some fled to Christian Abyssinia, in today's Ethiopia. In 619, the Year of Sorrow, both Khadija and Abu Talib died, and he was rejected and ridiculed in nearby Ta'if. Tradition then describes the Night Journey, when he traveled in one night to Jerusalem's al-Aqsa and ascended through the heavens, meeting earlier prophets like Abraham, Moses and Jesus.

Fourth, the Hijrah. In 622, Muhammad and his followers migrated to Medina, the starting point of the Islamic calendar. The Constitution of Medina bound together the city's tribes. Conflict with the Quraysh followed: the Muslims won a decisive victory at the Battle of Badr, which many saw as divine support, held off the Quraysh at Uhud, and eventually conquered Mecca.

Finally, the end of his life. With Mecca under Muslim control, he led the pilgrimage and delivered his Farewell Sermon. He fell ill and died in 632, and was buried in the Prophet's Mosque in Medina, which he had built.
