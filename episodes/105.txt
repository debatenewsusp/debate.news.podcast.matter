Episode 105. China's Internal Politics.
Today: how power works inside the Chinese Communist Party, its factions, and how Xi Jinping took control.

First, structure. Power sits in the Politburo, about two dozen top party members, and above all in its seven-member Standing Committee, effectively the most powerful body in China, from which every president and premier has come. Members are chosen through informal consultation among current and retired leaders. The Organization Department vets and promotes cadres, which makes it the engine of patronage: leaders lift their loyal protégés as they rise.

The party keeps legitimacy in two ways: delivering prosperity and running patronage networks. And it relies on four pillars: the internal security apparatus, the People's Liberation Army, which answers to the party rather than the state, state-owned enterprises, which steer the economy and reward loyalists, and the Publicity Department, which runs propaganda.

Second, factions. The Shanghai Gang formed around Jiang Zemin, a former Shanghai mayor, and favored the wealthy coastal cities, which received the bulk of foreign investment under Jiang; it is associated with elite insiders, including princelings, the descendants of revolutionary heroes. The Communist Youth League faction, or Tuanpai, rose under Hu Jintao, with a more populist focus on poorer inland regions. For years the two shared power, alternating top posts and producing hybrid policies that served different regions. Then came Xi's own faction: officials tied to him personally and fully loyal, favoring a tougher security and foreign policy, the crackdown on Hong Kong, curbs on private business, repression of dissent, and a push to end rural poverty.

Third, how Xi broke the power-sharing system. He used a sweeping anti-corruption campaign to purge rivals, including Politburo members and top generals. He cultivated a dual image, as the son of a revolutionary leader and as a man who did hard labor in the countryside during the Cultural Revolution, undercutting both the elite and the populist factions. He launched a massive campaign against rural poverty to steal the Youth League's issue. And he used state media to paint the other factions as corrupt and elitist. By his third term in 2022, the Standing Committee was filled with his loyalists.
