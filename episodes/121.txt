Episode 121. Niger.
Today: Niger's history of coups, the July 2023 putsch, and the regional reaction.

First, background. Niger is a Muslim-majority, French-speaking country with Hausa, Zarma and Tuareg communities, independent from France since 1960. Its history is a cycle of coups: in 1974, 1996, 1999 and 2010, sometimes followed by elections. Mahamadou Issoufou governed democratically from 2011, and in 2021 Mohamed Bazoum succeeded him, Niger's first peaceful democratic transfer of power, despite a failed coup attempt days before his inauguration.

Second, the 2023 coup. On July 26, General Abdourahamane Tchiani, head of the presidential guard, detained Bazoum in the presidential palace. That night the heads of all branches of the armed forces appeared on television backing the junta, which called itself the National Council for the Safeguard of the Homeland. Tchiani justified it by worsening security and bad governance in a country where two in five people live in extreme poverty. Many suspected he moved because Bazoum planned to remove him. Supporters waved Russian flags and Prigozhin praised the coup.

Third, context. Since 2015 Niger has fought jihadist groups linked to al-Qaeda and the Islamic State, with help from about eleven hundred U.S. troops at a base in Agadez and French forces. The junta accused France of plotting an invasion, tapping into anger at the cost of living and the French military presence.

Fourth, the reaction. ECOWAS, the West African bloc, had already suspended Mali, Guinea and Burkina Faso after their coups. It imposed sweeping sanctions, froze assets, closed borders and threatened military intervention, citing precedents from Liberia and Sierra Leone in the nineties to Gambia in 2017. France and the EU cut aid. Many feared a coup belt stretching across the Sahel from Sudan to Guinea.

Since the file was written: ECOWAS never intervened, French and then U.S. troops withdrew, and Niger joined Mali and Burkina Faso in a new Alliance of Sahel States and left ECOWAS, turning toward Russia.

That was Niger. Next episode: Ecuador.
