Episode 102. Twitter Under Musk.
Today: what changed after Elon Musk bought Twitter for forty-four billion dollars in 2022.

First, the turmoil. Top executives quit or were fired, about half the staff was laid off, thousands of advertisers left and revenue fell sharply, with the company reportedly losing millions a day while carrying about thirteen billion dollars of debt from the purchase.

Second, the main changes. Twitter went private, ending quarterly reporting and some oversight. Verification check marks became a paid subscription, which unleashed impersonators; a fake Eli Lilly account promising free insulin knocked billions off the company's market value. Musk, calling himself a free speech advocate, loosened content moderation. Advertisers like Volkswagen and United Airlines left, and Musk blamed activist groups like the Anti-Defamation League. He pursued new revenue, like paid messaging and payments. And he reinstated Donald Trump's account, banned after January 6.

Since then: Twitter was renamed X in 2023, and in 2025 it was merged into Musk's artificial intelligence company, xAI.

That was Twitter Under Musk. Next episode: Post Conflict Societies.
