Episode 127. Ethiopia.
Today: the Tigray war.

First, background. In 1991, a coalition of leftist rebels, the EPRDF, overthrew Ethiopia's brutal military junta. It was led by the Tigray People's Liberation Front, the TPLF, representing the Tigrayans, about six percent of the population in the far north. The TPLF built a federal system giving regions significant autonomy, and dominated government until 2018.

Second, Abiy Ahmed. Protests in 2018 brought Abiy to power as prime minister. He removed Tigrayans from top posts, arrested journalists and cracked down on Tigray. When the federal government postponed elections in 2020 because of COVID, Tigray held its own anyway, deepening the rift.

Third, the war. In November 2020, Abiy sent the army into Tigray, saying the TPLF had attacked a federal base. All sides were accused of war crimes, including airstrikes, massacres and sexual violence. The government cut communications, blocked observers and aid, and the fighting spilled across borders, with Eritrean troops joining on the government side.

Since the file was written: in November 2022, the African Union brokered a peace deal in Pretoria that ended the war, after hundreds of thousands of deaths. Tensions remain, including splits within the TPLF and conflict in the Amhara region.

That was Ethiopia. Next episode: Ukraine.
