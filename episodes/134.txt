Episode 134. Japan.
Today: Japan's history, politics, economy and security, starting the Asia section of the file.

First, history. Under the Tokugawa shogunate from the seventeenth century, Japan was isolated and ruled by a samurai class. In 1853, U.S. Commodore Perry's gunboats forced it open to trade. Japan responded with the Meiji Restoration of 1868: industrialization and militarization under the slogan rich nation, strong army. Reformist samurai modernized the state, conglomerates called zaibatsu like Mitsubishi worked with the government, and Japan built railways, telegraphs, steel mills and mass education. It became Asia's leading power, defeated Russia, and colonized Korea, Taiwan and Manchuria, with atrocities like the Nanjing Massacre, sexual slavery of so-called comfort women and Unit 731.

Second, the postwar order. After Hiroshima and Nagasaki, the U.S. occupation imposed a constitution whose Article 9 renounces war and armed forces. The U.S. then rebuilt Japan's economy with aid and Korean War purchases, while the state steered credit to big conglomerates and kept the yen cheap to boost exports, producing the economic miracle.

Third, politics. The Liberal Democratic Party has ruled almost continuously since 1955. Japan remains conservative: same-sex marriage is not legal, the population is about ninety-eight percent ethnically Japanese, and governments have been reluctant to fully acknowledge wartime crimes. Former prime minister Shinzo Abe was assassinated in 2022 by a man angry at Abe's ties to the Unification Church, a controversial religious movement founded in South Korea.

Fourth, the economy. After decades of rapid growth, a huge stock and property bubble, inflated by easy money after the 1985 Plaza Accord, burst around 1990, bringing bank failures and long deflation. Abenomics answered with three arrows: monetary easing, including negative interest rates and massive quantitative easing, fiscal stimulus and structural reform. Japan still faces stagnation and a shrinking population, falling by about eight hundred thousand in 2022.

Fifth, security. In 2014 Abe reinterpreted Article 9 to allow collective self-defense of allies, and in 2022 Japan began a five-year military buildup. Challenges include recruiting in a shrinking, aging society and a force that is only about eight percent female. Japan faces China over the Senkaku Islands, Chinese and Russian joint patrols, and North Korean missiles, and has a separate island dispute with South Korea over Dokdo, called Takeshima in Japan. It is deepening ties with the U.S., Europe and Asian partners.

Since the file was written: Fumio Kishida was replaced by Shigeru Ishiba in 2024, and in 2025 Sanae Takaichi, a hardline Abe ally, became Japan's first woman prime minister, governing with support from the right-wing Ishin party. The Bank of Japan has also ended negative interest rates.

That was Japan. Next episode: South Korea.
