Episode 125. Sudan.
Today: how Sudan's army and its main paramilitary force ended up at war.

First, background. Sudan fought two long civil wars after independence in 1956, the second ending with South Sudan's independence in 2011. Meanwhile, President Omar al-Bashir's army waged genocide in Darfur, alongside the Janjaweed militia under General Hemedti. Bashir later turned the Janjaweed into an official force, the Rapid Support Forces, or RSF, and gave Hemedti lucrative gold mines.

Second, the failed transition. Mass protests from December 2018 led the military to remove Bashir in April 2019. After the RSF massacred protesters in Khartoum that June, international pressure forced a power-sharing council with civilians until elections. But in October 2021, army chief General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan seized power in another coup.

Third, the war. The army then demanded the RSF merge into it within two years, and eyed Hemedti's gold and possible prosecution for Darfur. The RSF wanted ten years. Tensions exploded into civil war in April 2023, with Khartoum devastated. The UAE is accused of arming the RSF under cover of humanitarian aid, while its rival Saudi Arabia backs the army.

Since the file was written: the war has created the world's largest displacement crisis and widespread famine. The army retook Khartoum in 2025, while the RSF captured El Fasher in Darfur, amid reports of mass atrocities, leaving the country effectively split.

That was Sudan. Next episode: South Sudan.
