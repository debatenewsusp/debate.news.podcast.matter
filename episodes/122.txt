Episode 122. Ecuador.
Today: Ecuador's surge in gang violence, the assassination of a presidential candidate, and its political crisis.

First, the violence. Ecuador sits between Colombia and Peru, the world's main cocaine producers. Since 2016, murders rose around fivefold to over twenty per hundred thousand. Prisons became battlegrounds, with over a hundred inmates killed in a single massacre in Guayaquil in 2021. The causes: FARC's demobilization in Colombia left a vacuum that Mexican cartels and the Albanian mafia filled; gangs spread after the 2016 earthquake; COVID and falling oil prices deepened poverty; IMF-backed austerity sparked unrest and slashed spending; President Rafael Correa ended cooperation with the U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration; and corruption reached high levels of the state. The main gangs are the Choneros, whose leader's killing in 2020 sparked a bloody split, and the Lobos.

Second, the assassination. On August 9, 2023, presidential candidate Fernando Villavicencio, an outspoken anti-corruption campaigner who had tied gangs to officials and received threats from the Choneros' leader, known as Fito, was shot dead after a rally in Quito. Colombian nationals were arrested.

Third, the political crisis. President Guillermo Lasso, elected in 2021, faced the Pandora Papers revelations about offshore accounts, large Indigenous protests over fuel and food prices, and allegations of mafia links. Facing impeachment in 2023, he dissolved the National Assembly, triggering early elections.

Since the file was written: the young businessman Daniel Noboa won the presidency in 2023. In January 2024, after Fito escaped from prison and gunmen stormed a TV studio live on air, Noboa declared an internal armed conflict against the gangs, and he won a full term in 2025.
