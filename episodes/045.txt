Episode 45. Art Museums and Galleries.
Today: the big museums and the five main challenges they face.

First, the essentials. Major museums include the Louvre, the British Museum and Tate Modern, the Met and MoMA in New York, the Prado, the Uffizi, the Hermitage and the Rijksmuseum.

Challenge one: money. Staff and operating costs are huge, and ticket revenue rarely covers them, because raising prices drives visitors away and only a few museums draw millions. So museums depend on government funding, donations, corporate sponsors and wealthy patrons.

Challenge two: ethically dubious donors. The Sackler family, whose company Purdue Pharma profited from the opioid crisis, has its name across major museums. The Whitney's vice chair resigned after protests over his company selling tear gas. BP sponsored the British Museum for decades until 2023.

Challenge three: poor representation. Many museums began as private collections of the elite, like the Medici's Uffizi, and displayed loot from colonies. Visitors skew wealthy and educated, so museums cater to them, while staff are mostly white and exhibits often only in English. Breaking into art depends on connections and elite academies, so the canon was shaped by white Western men: for decades a tiny share of the Met's art was by women, while most nudes depicted women, and Black artists get a sliver of the auction market. Even progressive gestures often amount to virtue signaling for the wealthy.

Challenge four: museums are political. Curators choose which stories to tell. States use museums to project power: the Louvre opened after the French Revolution to win support for the Republic, and Napoleon used it to display his plunder. Ownership itself is political, as with the British Museum's Elgin Marbles and Rosetta Stone. Pressure is working, though: the Smithsonian returned Benin bronzes to Nigeria, and New York prosecutors seized looted artifacts from the Met.

Challenge five: falling attendance after COVID and competition from digital entertainment, though most visits come from a small core of art lovers.
