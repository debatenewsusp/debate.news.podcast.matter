Episode 114. Armenia and Azerbaijan.
Today: profiles of both countries and the conflict over Nagorno-Karabakh.

First, Armenia. Capital Yerevan, almost entirely ethnic Armenian, and mostly members of the Armenian Apostolic Church; it was the first country to adopt Christianity, in the fourth century. Russia is its biggest trade partner, investor and gas supplier, and Armenia belongs to Russia's Eurasian Economic Union, though it grew fast in 2022 and receives large remittances from its diaspora. Politically, the 2018 Velvet Revolution forced out Serzh Sargsyan, accused of corruption and power grabs, and brought Nikol Pashinyan to power, who stayed in office even after losing the 2020 war. Armenia's border with Turkey has been closed since 1993, though normalization talks began in 2022, and relations with Russia soured as Armenia felt abandoned by the Russian-led security alliance and moved toward joining the International Criminal Court.

History: under Ottoman rule, Armenians were repeatedly massacred, culminating in the genocide during World War One, when over a million died. Armenia then spent seventy years in the Soviet Union until 1991.

Second, Azerbaijan. Capital Baku, mostly ethnic Azerbaijani and Shia Muslim, with an economy dominated by oil and gas, about ninety percent of exports, run by the state company SOCAR. Heydar Aliyev, a former Soviet KGB chief, ruled as a strongman from 1993 and handed power to his son Ilham in 2003, who has won repeated elections marred by fraud and intimidation after removing term limits. Oil wealth fuels corruption, including a multibillion-dollar laundering scheme to buy influence among European politicians. It is a close ally of Turkey, and in 2022 the EU signed a deal to double its gas imports from Azerbaijan.

Third, Nagorno-Karabakh, an ethnically Armenian region inside Azerbaijan's internationally recognized borders, run for decades by the breakaway Republic of Artsakh. Armenia won the first war in the early nineties. Azerbaijan, backed by Turkey and its drones, won the second in 2020, and Russia brokered a ceasefire with Russian peacekeepers. With Russia distracted by Ukraine, Azerbaijan blockaded the Lachin corridor, the only road linking the region to Armenia.

Since the file was written: in September 2023 Azerbaijan seized the whole region in a lightning offensive, more than a hundred thousand Armenians fled, and the breakaway republic dissolved. In 2025 the two countries' leaders signed a U.S.-brokered peace declaration at the White House.

That was Armenia and Azerbaijan. Next episode: Yemen.
