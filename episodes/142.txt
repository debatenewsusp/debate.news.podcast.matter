Episode 142. Indonesia.
Today: from Sukarno and Suharto to democracy, plus Indonesia's economy and foreign policy.

First, history. Indonesia declared independence from the Netherlands in August 1945 and won it after four years of fighting, led by Sukarno. After unstable parliamentary rule, he imposed Guided Democracy in 1959, while inflation exceeded a thousand percent. After a failed coup by junior officers in 1965, General Suharto blamed the communists and led a massacre of hundreds of thousands of suspected leftists, taking full power by 1967. His New Order was a corrupt military dictatorship, but its anti-communism won Western support and rapid industrialization. The 1997 Asian financial crisis devastated the economy, protests forced Suharto out in 1998, and Indonesia became a multiparty democracy.

Second, politics. President Joko Widodo, known as Jokowi, of the center-left PDI-P, governed from 2014 to 2024. Corruption remains widespread, and governments often bring opposition parties into the cabinet, which slows decisions.

Third, the economy. Indonesia is ASEAN's largest economy and most populous country. It relies on exporting coal and palm oil, but is moving into electric vehicle batteries using its huge nickel reserves. Weaknesses include a weak rupiah, corruption, red tape and a largely low-skilled workforce.

Fourth, foreign policy. Indonesia is non-aligned. China is its largest trade partner, it stayed neutral on Ukraine and keeps ties with Russia, yet it courts Western investment. With Malaysia, it signed a 2023 deal on border disputes, and both oppose the EU's deforestation law as discriminatory against palm oil. It works with Australia on the green transition, pairing Indonesian nickel with Australian lithium. A new criminal code banning sex outside marriage and criticism of the government worries Western partners.

Since the file was written: former general Prabowo Subianto won the February 2024 election with Jokowi's son as vice president, and Indonesia joined the BRICS group in 2025. Large protests in 2025 over lawmakers' perks and inequality challenged his government.
